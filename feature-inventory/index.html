Every row below was checked against the deposit itself, not the paper's claim about it. Three earlier statements changed as a result: the PPARγ HDX deposit does contain processed per-compound uptake rather than raw files only; the NIST ERK2 trajectories are reachable again after the earlier server errors; and the ERK2 inhibitor panel, not Aurora A HDX, is the largest ligand-resolved dynamics set available.

Access blockers worth an email

ERK2 inhibitor structures — Anderson et al. 2024

The HDX workbook is public and complete, but inhibitors #1–17 appear only as drawings in Figure 4. Without machine-readable structures, 17 of the 21 compounds cannot be modelled. This single request would unlock the top-ranked dataset.

PPARγ SR-series structures — Kuo et al.

23 compounds have processed HDX and matched functional data, but only SR10221 resolves to a structure in PubChem.

Aurora A single-molecule dwell times — Zenodo

Records 825693, 825752 and 2555379 return 403 to this network through the API, the DOI resolver and the export route alike. This is the only public source of state lifetimes, which is what Arm 4 needs.

GCK HDX-MS — Sternisha et al. 2018

The ACS supplementary returns 403, the PubMed Central record carries no supplementary files, and no proteomics deposit exists. GCK has no usable HDX data today.

Counts of structures, ligands and resolutions come from the RCSB data API over the 870 entries in our catalogue, classified by excluding ions, buffers and cryoprotectants and treating components under 150 Da as non-ligands. Compound, peptide and time-point counts come from the deposited files themselves, downloaded and parsed. Rights are as stated by each archive; the OSF Aurora A node states no licence.
